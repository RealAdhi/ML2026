*(Source: `results/analysis/error_types_xgboost.csv`, compared across models in `results/analysis/error_types.png`)*

### Which Error Type Dominates and Why?
`reverse_direction` overwhelmingly dominates, accounting for nearly half (**47.12%**) of all errors committed by XGBoost. This aligns directly with findings in `README.md` Section 3.5 and 4: road networks model two-way streets as two parallel directed edges with identical coordinates. For 65.1% of GPS fixes, the two nearest arcs are tied in distance. When vehicle speed drops or GPS jitter induces slight heading errors, single-point classifier features (`cos_h`, `abs_ang`) fail to break the symmetry, causing the model to pick the reverse lane. The second most frequent error is `adjacent_arc` (**33.53%**), which arises near intersections or when a vehicle transitions between contiguous segments, where the orthogonal projection point briefly falls onto the neighboring road segment.

## 3. Probability Calibration and Relevance to HMM

Probability calibration curves were evaluated on all reachable candidates (159,878 rows) across 10 probability bins (`results/analysis/calibration.png` and `results/analysis/pr_curves.png`).

### Observations per Model
1. **XGBoost (AP = 0.9465)**: Demonstrates near-ideal calibration across all bins, hugging the $y = x$ diagonal within 1–2% mean deviation. Its predicted probabilities reliably reflect true empirical likelihoods.
2. **Random Forest (AP = 0.9443)**: Well-calibrated with slight underconfidence in the highest confidence decile (> 0.90 predicted probability yields ~0.97 observed precision).
3. **LightGBM (AP = 0.9293)**: Moderately well-calibrated, though showing minor compression towards the mean in the 0.50–0.70 confidence band.
4. **MLP Neural Network (AP = 0.9400)**: Displays noticeable miscalibration in the upper decile, where predicted probabilities around 0.85 only yield an observed precision of 0.83.
5. **Logistic Regression (AP = 0.9040)**: Exhibits pronounced overconfidence in the mid-range (0.15–0.60 probability region), where predicted probabilities consistently exceed the empirical positive rate by 5–8 percentage points.

### Why Calibration is Critical for the HMM
In Hidden Markov Model route reconstruction, Viterbi decoding computes the globally optimal trajectory by maximizing the joint score $\sum_{t} \ln P(z_t \mid c_{t, i}) + \ln P(c_{t, j} \mid c_{t-1, i})$. The emission probability scale directly balances against the transition penalty $\beta$. If a classifier is poorly calibrated and outputs overconfident probabilities for spurious candidates, the emission term will overpower the network transition penalty, forcing the decoder to accept physically impossible teleportations between disconnected roads. Calibrated probabilities ensure that when spatial uncertainty is high, the road network transition priors can smoothly override candidate noise.

Based on model training logs and candidate design (`src/pathsnap/candidates.py`), the top 8 most influential features for candidate discrimination are:
1. **`cos_h`**: Cosine of the angle between GPS displacement bearing and arc orientation; essential for disambiguating two-way road directions.
2. **`d`**: Perpendicular distance from GPS fix to arc segment; primary spatial proximity indicator.
3. **`d_rank`**: Ordinal rank of candidate by distance (0 = nearest); isolates the dominant physical candidate.
4. **`d_vs_best_dir`**: Difference between candidate distance and the distance to the closest direction-consistent arc; breaks ties when the nearest arc has wrong heading.
5. **`d_prev` / `d_next`**: Distance of previous and subsequent GPS fixes to the candidate segment; provides temporal context without sequence modeling.
6. **`frac`**: Normalized projection position along the arc (0.0 to 1.0); prevents matching fixes that lie past the arc endpoints.
7. **`arc_len`**: Physical length of the segment; accounts for projection bias on short vs. long road segments.
8. **`n_dir_ok`**: Number of candidates agreeing with trajectory direction; indicates junction complexity and directional ambiguity.

## 5. Concrete Improvements for Future Iterations

Based on the quantitative error counts recorded in `results/analysis/`, we propose five targeted improvements:

1. **Multi-Fix Smoothed Trajectory Bearing (Target: `reverse_direction`, 47.1% of errors)**:
   * *Evidence*: 1,213 errors were `reverse_direction` caused by instantaneous GPS noise flipping the single-step bearing $\Delta \theta$.
   * *Improvement*: Compute trajectory bearing using a rolling window of 3–5 fixes ($\Delta t \approx 3\text{–}5\text{s}$) to filter out low-speed jitter on dual-carriageway roads.
2. **Boundary Proximity Interaction Term (Target: `adjacent_arc`, 33.5% of errors)**:
   * *Evidence*: 863 errors were `adjacent_arc` occurring within 10 meters of node intersections.
   * *Improvement*: Add an interaction feature $\min(d_{\text{to\_start}}, d_{\text{to\_end}}) / (\text{speed} \cdot \Delta t + 1)$ to model handoff probability between connected segments.
3. **Speed-to-Road Hierarchy Consistency Feature (Target: `other`, 17.8% of errors)**:
   * *Evidence*: 459 errors selected non-adjacent arcs, predominantly during high-speed segments in Trip 77.
   * *Improvement*: Add a feature scoring the discrepancy between observed vehicle speed and expected road capacity/degree (`deg_from`, `outdeg_to`), preventing highway traces from matching onto parallel frontage roads.
4. **Dynamic Spatial Search Radius Expansion (Target: `candidate_miss`, 1.5% of errors)**:
   * *Evidence*: 39 fixes had zero valid candidates within the fixed 60m radius.
   * *Improvement*: If all candidates within 60m have $\cos_h < 0$ or no candidates are found, dynamically expand candidate retrieval to 100m to capture wide highway overpasses and separated carriageways.
5. **Speed-Adaptive Emission Temperature Scaling (Target: Trip 77 Anomaly, 44.2% of all errors)**:
   * *Evidence*: High-speed runs exhibit wider lateral variance and higher noise.
   * *Improvement*: Scale candidate prediction logits by an inverse temperature $\tau(v) = 1 + \alpha \cdot \text{speed}$ before feeding into the HMM, flattening emission peaks at high velocity to let network transition topology guide the path.
